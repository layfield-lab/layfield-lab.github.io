Research for St. Thomas undergraduates

You don't need programming experience to start. You need curiosity, steady weekly time, and a willingness to check your work. We teach the rest.

What you'll do

  • Join one of the lab's research projects and own a piece of it.
  • Run quantum chemistry or molecular dynamics calculations on shared computing clusters.
  • Analyze results in Python and keep a clear record of what you ran and why.
  • Present your work at the UROP Inquiry poster session, and at regional or national meetings when the work is ready.
  • Contribute to open-source research software and, when you've earned it, co-author papers.

What we expect

  • Regular weekly hours and a weekly check-in with Dr. Layfield.
  • Asking questions early, before a problem costs a week of computer time.
  • Checking every calculation before you analyze it.
  • Writing up your work in the structure of a journal article.

How you'll learn

  1. Foundation. Python for chemists.
  2. Quantum chemistry track. Reading and analyzing Gaussian output, structure alignment and thermochemistry.
  3. Molecular dynamics track. Trajectory analysis with MDAnalysis, including a real enhanced-sampling simulation of a tetrapeptide.
  4. Enhanced sampling track. PLUMED, neural networks and machine-learned collective variables.

All notebooks run in Google Colab with nothing to install. They're open to anyone: chem-lab-tutorials.

Funding for student research

Students can be paid for research through St. Thomas programs, including UROP grants for academic-year and summer projects and the Young Scholars program, and on some projects through external grants. We'll plan an application together.

How to get started

Email Dr. Josh Layfield at jlayfield@stthomas.edu. Include your year and major, which research area interests you, and roughly how many hours a week you could commit, and we'll set up a time to talk.
